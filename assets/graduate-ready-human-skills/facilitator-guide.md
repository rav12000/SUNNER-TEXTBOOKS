# Graduate-Ready Human Skills Facilitator Guide

## Purpose
This guide supports facilitators, tutors, student services teams and employability staff to deliver **Graduate-Ready Human Skills** as an orientation intensive, eight-week employability module, placement preparation companion or self-guided portfolio program.

## Professional boundaries
The program is psycho-educational and employability-focused. It is not therapy, crisis intervention, clinical supervision, legal advice or formal workplace compliance training. Facilitators should follow institutional safeguarding, student conduct, academic integrity, placement and referral policies.

## Facilitator stance
Use a grounded, practical and inclusive tone. The best facilitation style for this resource is:

- **Specific:** ask for observable behaviours rather than vague confidence claims.
- **Choice-based:** allow private reflection and opt-in sharing.
- **Evidence-led:** connect every discussion to portfolio artefacts.
- **Trauma-aware:** do not require disclosure of distress, hardship or personal history.
- **Institution-aware:** verify local graduate attributes, employability frameworks and student support pathways before delivery.

## Opening script
Welcome everyone. This program is about building practical human skills for study, placement, work and leadership. We will focus on communication, resilience, self-leadership, teamwork, initiative and ethical judgement. You will not be asked to share anything deeply personal. Use examples that feel appropriate and protect other people's privacy. If anything raises a wellbeing concern for you, please use the support pathways available through the institution or speak with a trusted staff member.

## Core facilitation rhythm
Each session follows a repeatable structure:

1. **Settle and frame** — remind participants of the purpose and boundaries.
2. **Scenario** — discuss a realistic graduate situation.
3. **Concept** — teach one practical framework.
4. **Practice** — complete an individual, pair or group task.
5. **Portfolio output** — create an artefact students can keep.
6. **Reflection** — name one behaviour to practise before the next session.

## Eight-week delivery plan
| Week | Chapter | Session purpose | Main activity | Portfolio output | Facilitator watchpoint |
|---:|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The Human Skills Advantage | Reframe employability as evidenced behaviour | Evidence map workshop | Human skills evidence map | Students may use generic claims; ask for examples |
| 2 | Self-Leadership and Professional Identity | Build values-based professional language | Professional profile clinic | 150-200 word profile | Avoid one-size-fits-all professionalism |
| 3 | Communication in Professional Settings | Practise clear updates and requests | Rewrite unclear messages | Email/update templates | Support multilingual and neurodiverse communication styles |
| 4 | Teamwork and Collaboration | Design collaboration before conflict occurs | Team charter simulation | Team charter | Acknowledge prior unfair group-work experiences |
| 5 | Resilience Under Pressure | Build humane pressure-response habits | Pressure map and support planning | Personal pressure plan | Keep sharing choice-based and non-clinical |
| 6 | Initiative, Ownership and Self-Management | Improve planning and progress communication | Prioritisation scenario | Four-week self-management log | Distinguish ownership from self-blame |
| 7 | Ethical Decision-Making and Values | Practise values-led judgement | PAUSE scenario cards | Ethical judgement statement | Refer to policy; do not provide legal advice |
| 8 | Graduate Readiness Portfolio | Curate evidence for real opportunities | Peer portfolio review | Final action plan | Protect privacy in artefacts |

## Session timing template: 90 minutes
| Time | Segment | Notes |
|---:|---|---|
| 0-5 min | Welcome and boundaries | Name purpose, support pathways and opt-in sharing |
| 5-15 min | Scenario discussion | Use the textbook scenario or a local example |
| 15-30 min | Concept teaching | Keep slides concise and practical |
| 30-50 min | Individual worksheet | Quiet writing before group work |
| 50-70 min | Pair/group practice | Use roles, time limits and clear outputs |
| 70-82 min | Portfolio output | Students create or improve one artefact |
| 82-90 min | Reflection and close | One behaviour to practise; preview next session |

## Adaptations
### First-year orientation
Focus on Chapters 1, 2, 3 and 5. Use low-risk examples from study, casual work and community life. Keep outputs simple and confidence-building.

### Work-integrated learning preparation
Emphasise Chapters 3, 4, 6 and 7. Add local placement policies, confidentiality rules, supervisor communication expectations and escalation pathways.

### Residential college or student leadership
Emphasise Chapters 1, 2, 4, 5 and 7. Add community standards, peer leadership, conflict repair and help-seeking pathways.

### Online asynchronous delivery
Use short video introductions, downloadable worksheets and discussion prompts. Ask students to upload one portfolio artefact per module.

## Debrief question bank
- What behaviour built trust in that example?
- What would be visible to another person?
- What assumption might create risk here?
- What is the smallest useful next action?
- What evidence could a student keep from this experience?
- What policy, person or support pathway should be checked before acting?

## Safeguarding and referral language
If a student appears distressed, do not ask them to disclose details in front of the group. You may say privately: “Thank you for letting me know. This program is not designed to provide counselling, but I can help connect you with the right support pathway. Would you like information about student wellbeing support or another trusted contact?” Follow institutional policy.
