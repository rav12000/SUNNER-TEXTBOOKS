# Resilient Teams Facilitator Guide

## Purpose

This guide supports delivery of **Resilient Teams: A Practical Training Manual for Adaptability, Grit and Trust** in half-day, full-day and modular formats.

## Facilitation stance

- Keep the work practical, behavioural and workplace-focused.
- Do not invite clinical disclosures or position the session as therapy.
- Encourage use of internal support channels, EAP, HR, managers, WHS contacts, union or representative support, and qualified professionals where needed.
- Make participation respectful: people may pass on personal examples and use general workplace scenarios instead.

## Opening script

“Today is a practical team development session. We are focusing on behaviours, communication, trust, adaptability and sustainable performance. This is not therapy, legal advice or a replacement for HR, safety or clinical support. You can participate without sharing private personal information. If something raises a support need, use the appropriate workplace or professional pathway.”

## Activity instructions and debrief prompts

### Team resilience diagnostic

1. Ask participants to score privately.
2. Invite pairs to compare themes, not individual scores.
3. Capture common strengths and focus areas.

Debrief: “Which behaviour would create the most benefit if practised consistently for 30 days?”

### Pressure pattern map

1. Groups list common pressure triggers.
2. They name default reactions without blame.
3. They write better responses as observable behaviours.

Debrief: “What is one early signal that tells us pressure is rising?”

### Values to standards

1. Choose three values.
2. Translate each value into visible behaviour.
3. Add a pressure test and a non-example.

Debrief: “Would a new starter understand this standard after reading it once?”

### CLEAR update practice

1. Select a current priority.
2. Complete Context, Limit, Expectation, Action and Review.
3. Practise delivering the update in under two minutes.

Debrief: “What ambiguity did this structure remove?”

### Repair conversation rehearsal

1. Use low-risk examples.
2. Practise the five-part repair structure.
3. Discuss escalation boundaries.

Debrief: “When would informal repair be inappropriate, and what formal pathway should be used instead?”

## Group dynamics notes

- If the group is quiet, begin with individual writing before discussion.
- If one person dominates, ask for two voices that have not spoken yet.
- If conflict escalates, pause the activity, restate boundaries and move to process rather than content.
- If participants seek HR/legal advice, redirect to organisational policies and qualified advice.

## Risk and HR boundary notes

This training may surface workload, safety, interpersonal or wellbeing concerns. The facilitator should not investigate, diagnose or adjudicate. Capture themes at a high level only, and encourage participants to use appropriate reporting, HR, WHS, EAP or professional support channels.
