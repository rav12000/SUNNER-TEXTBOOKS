# Resilient Teams

## A Practical Training Manual for Adaptability, Grit and Trust

**Participant manual**  
Prepared for workplace teams, managers, emerging leaders, frontline teams, small businesses, community organisations and staff wellbeing programs.

### Title page copy

Resilient Teams is a practical training manual for teams that want to build dependable habits under pressure. It helps participants turn shared values into visible behaviour, communicate more clearly when work is complex, recover from conflict, set sustainable boundaries and create a team resilience charter that can be used after the workshop.

### Professional disclaimer

This manual is a training and professional development resource. It is not therapy, clinical mental health treatment, legal advice, HR compliance advice or crisis intervention. Participants should use appropriate workplace support channels, such as their manager, HR, workplace health and safety contacts, EAP, union or representative support, and qualified medical or mental health professionals where needed. If anyone is at immediate risk, follow local emergency procedures.

### About the facilitator

Harman Sunner's product ecosystem draws on facilitation, public speaking, counselling-informed psycho-educational group work, meditation, journaling, self-reflection, resilience workshops and small business experience. The tone of this manual is practical, grounded and experiential: teams are invited to discuss real work patterns, practise new behaviours and leave with specific commitments.

### How to use this manual

Use this manual as a participant workbook during a half-day, full-day or multi-session team development program. Each module includes a short concept explanation, a workplace scenario, discussion prompts, a team exercise, a worksheet, facilitator notes, behaviour commitments and a workplace application challenge. Managers can use the implementation roadmap to embed the learning in meetings, one-on-ones and team rituals.

### Learning outcomes

By the end of the program, participants will be able to:

1. Describe the everyday behaviours that make teams more resilient.
2. Translate values into agreed team standards.
3. Build trust through clarity, reliability, repair and support.
4. Communicate more effectively under pressure.
5. Adapt to change without losing purpose or care.
6. Recover from conflict through respectful repair processes.
7. Set boundaries that protect performance, wellbeing and accountability.
8. Create and implement a practical team resilience charter.

# Module 1: What resilient teams do differently

## Module purpose

To define resilience as a set of team behaviours rather than a personality trait or motivational slogan.

## Learning outcomes

Participants will identify the difference between individual toughness and team resilience, recognise patterns that help teams recover, and choose one resilience habit to practise.

## Workplace scenario

A hybrid project team misses a client deadline after two people are away, priorities shift twice and messages are spread across email, chat and meetings. The team works hard, but the recovery conversation becomes defensive. Everyone wants improvement, yet no one is sure which behaviour to change first.

## Concept explanation

Resilient teams are not teams that avoid stress. They are teams that notice pressure early, communicate clearly, support each other appropriately, adapt their plan and repair trust when things go wrong. Resilience is built through repeatable habits: clarity before speed, support before isolation, reflection before blame and recovery before burnout.

## Discussion activity

In pairs, discuss:

- When has this team handled pressure well?
- What behaviour made the difference?
- When pressure rises, what do we tend to stop doing?

## Team exercise: Pressure pattern map

Create three columns: **Pressure trigger**, **Default reaction**, **Better team response**. List common triggers such as urgent client requests, staff shortages, unclear ownership, late changes or technology issues. Choose one trigger and define a better response in one sentence.

## Worksheet: Resilience habit selector

| Current pressure pattern | Helpful replacement habit | Owner | First test date |
|---|---|---|---|
|  |  |  |  |

## Manager/facilitator note

Keep the conversation behavioural. Avoid asking participants to disclose personal mental health information. Invite examples about work systems, communication and team rituals.

## Behaviour commitments

- We will name pressure early.
- We will clarify what matters most before reacting.
- We will review misses for learning, not blame.

## Workplace application challenge

At the next team meeting, spend ten minutes reviewing one recent pressure point using the pressure pattern map.

# Module 2: Shared values and team standards

## Module purpose

To turn broad values into observable standards that guide everyday decisions.

## Learning outcomes

Participants will distinguish values from slogans, define team standards and agree on behaviours that show values in action.

## Workplace scenario

A small business says it values respect, but staff experience respect differently. One person sees direct feedback as respectful; another experiences it as abrupt. Without agreed standards, the same value creates friction.

## Concept explanation

Values become useful when people can see them. A team standard answers: what will we do when this value is under pressure? For example, integrity may mean raising risks early, owning mistakes and documenting decisions. Care may mean checking capacity before assigning urgent work.

## Discussion activity

Choose three values that matter to your team. For each, complete: **When work is easy, this value looks like... When work is hard, this value looks like...**

## Team exercise: Values to standards

| Value | Visible behaviour | Pressure test | Non-example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Respect |  |  |  |
| Accountability |  |  |  |
| Care |  |  |  |

## Worksheet/template

Draft one team standard using this format: “Because we value ___, we will ___, especially when ___.”

## Manager/facilitator note

Encourage teams to write plain-language standards. Avoid corporate jargon that sounds impressive but does not change behaviour.

## Behaviour commitments

- We will make values observable.
- We will challenge behaviour against standards, not personalities.
- We will revisit standards when work changes.

## Workplace application challenge

Display the top five standards in the team's shared workspace or meeting agenda for one month.

# Module 3: Trust, psychological safety and support

## Module purpose

To build practical trust through reliability, candour, appropriate support and respectful challenge.

## Learning outcomes

Participants will identify trust-building behaviours, describe psychologically safer meeting habits without overclaiming expertise, and practise support-seeking language.

## Workplace scenario

A frontline team notices a process risk, but junior staff hesitate to speak because previous concerns were dismissed. The issue becomes bigger than necessary. The team does not need a slogan about safety; it needs meeting behaviours that make it easier to raise risks early.

## Concept explanation

Trust grows when people experience consistency: commitments are kept, context is shared, concerns are heard and mistakes are handled fairly. Psychologically safer teams do not guarantee comfort or agreement. They create conditions where respectful speaking up is more likely and where leaders respond constructively.

## Discussion activity

What makes it easier or harder to say “I need help”, “I made a mistake” or “I see a risk” in this team?

## Team exercise: Safer meeting moves

Practise three phrases:

- “What are we missing?”
- “Who has a different read on this?”
- “What support would make this workable?”

## Worksheet/template

| Trust deposit | Trust withdrawal | Repair action |
|---|---|---|
| Keeping commitments | Cancelling without context | Renegotiate early and explain impact |
|  |  |  |

## Manager/facilitator note

Do not invite disclosures of trauma or private health information. Keep the frame on workplace behaviours, leader responses and support pathways.

## Behaviour commitments

- We will ask for dissent before decisions are locked.
- We will treat early risk-raising as responsible behaviour.
- We will repair trust when commitments are missed.

## Workplace application challenge

Add “risks, blockers and support needed” to the next four team meeting agendas.

# Module 4: Communication under pressure

## Module purpose

To improve clarity, listening and decision communication when urgency is high.

## Learning outcomes

Participants will use a simple pressure communication structure, practise clarifying priorities and reduce avoidable miscommunication.

## Workplace scenario

A leadership team moves quickly during an operational issue. Decisions are made in side conversations, staff receive different versions of the priority and rework increases. The issue is not effort; it is communication discipline.

## Concept explanation

Pressure narrows attention. Teams need simple communication habits that reduce ambiguity: name the situation, state the priority, identify owners, agree the next check-in and confirm what is not being done now.

## Discussion activity

Where does communication usually break down for this team: assumptions, channels, timing, tone, ownership or follow-up?

## Team exercise: The CLEAR update

Use this structure:

- **Context:** What has changed?
- **Limit:** What constraint matters?
- **Expectation:** What is needed now?
- **Action:** Who owns what by when?
- **Review:** When will we check progress?

## Worksheet/template

| Context | Limit | Expectation | Action owner | Review point |
|---|---|---|---|---|
|  |  |  |  |  |

## Manager/facilitator note

Separate speed from vagueness. The goal is not longer communication; it is cleaner communication.

## Behaviour commitments

- We will confirm ownership and deadlines.
- We will use one source of truth for urgent updates.
- We will clarify what is paused when new priorities appear.

## Workplace application challenge

Use the CLEAR update for one live priority this week.

# Module 5: Adapting to change

## Module purpose

To help teams respond to change with purpose, learning and realistic planning.

## Learning outcomes

Participants will identify controllable and uncontrollable factors, use a change response map and convert uncertainty into next actions.

## Workplace scenario

An organisation introduces a new system. Some people are excited, some are frustrated and some quietly disengage. The team needs space to name concerns, learn the system and keep service standards steady.

## Concept explanation

Adaptability is not pretending change is easy. It is the disciplined process of noticing what has changed, naming what still matters, learning what is required and adjusting behaviour. Teams adapt better when they preserve purpose while changing methods.

## Discussion activity

What do we need to keep, stop, start or learn because of this change?

## Team exercise: Change response map

| What changed? | What still matters? | What can we control? | What support is needed? | Next experiment |
|---|---|---|---|---|
|  |  |  |  |  |

## Worksheet/template

Write one “next experiment” that can be tested within two weeks. Define what success would look like.

## Manager/facilitator note

Allow honest discussion without letting the session become a complaint loop. Move from naming impact to identifying agency.

## Behaviour commitments

- We will name change impacts honestly.
- We will protect purpose while adjusting method.
- We will test small experiments before demanding perfect solutions.

## Workplace application challenge

Run a 20-minute keep/stop/start/learn review for a current change.

# Module 6: Conflict, recovery and repair

## Module purpose

To normalise respectful repair as a core resilience skill.

## Learning outcomes

Participants will distinguish healthy disagreement from unhelpful conflict, practise repair language and define escalation pathways.

## Workplace scenario

Two departments disagree about response times. Emails become sharper, assumptions harden and meetings become performative. The work issue remains unsolved because the relationship tension is now driving the process.

## Concept explanation

Conflict is not automatically a sign of failure. Teams need disagreement to find risks and improve decisions. The risk is unmanaged conflict: indirect communication, blame, avoidance or personal attacks. Repair means returning to standards, naming impact, owning contribution and agreeing a next behaviour.

## Discussion activity

What conflict habits help this team solve problems? What habits make problems bigger?

## Team exercise: Repair conversation rehearsal

Use this structure:

1. “The situation I want to repair is...”
2. “The impact I noticed was...”
3. “My contribution may have been...”
4. “The standard I want us to return to is...”
5. “A useful next step would be...”

## Worksheet/template

| Tension | Shared standard | My contribution | Repair request | Follow-up date |
|---|---|---|---|---|
|  |  |  |  |  |

## Manager/facilitator note

This module is not a substitute for formal grievance, misconduct, safety or performance processes. Encourage appropriate escalation where there are serious concerns.

## Behaviour commitments

- We will address tension early and respectfully.
- We will own our contribution before demanding change from others.
- We will use formal pathways when informal repair is not appropriate.

## Workplace application challenge

Identify one low-risk tension or recurring frustration and use the repair structure to reset expectations.

# Module 7: Boundaries and sustainable performance

## Module purpose

To establish boundaries that protect focus, recovery, accountability and long-term performance.

## Learning outcomes

Participants will define healthy workplace boundaries, identify boundary leaks and agree norms for availability, workload and recovery.

## Workplace scenario

A high-performing team prides itself on responsiveness. Over time, “quick questions” after hours become normal, deep work disappears and people feel guilty taking breaks. The team needs boundaries that support excellent work rather than lower standards.

## Concept explanation

Boundaries are not barriers to teamwork. They are agreements that help people use energy, attention and time responsibly. Sustainable performance requires clarity about priorities, realistic capacity conversations, recovery practices and escalation when workload is unsafe or unmanageable.

## Discussion activity

Where do we need clearer boundaries: meetings, messages, response times, urgent requests, workload, emotional labour or decision rights?

## Team exercise: Boundary norm reset

Choose one area and define:

- What is reasonable?
- What is urgent?
- What should be escalated?
- What should wait?

## Worksheet/template

| Boundary area | Current norm | Preferred norm | Exception process | Review date |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| After-hours messages |  |  |  |  |
| Meetings |  |  |  |  |
| Workload |  |  |  |  |

## Manager/facilitator note

Boundary conversations must connect to operational reality. Avoid implying that individuals can solve systemic workload issues through mindset alone.

## Behaviour commitments

- We will clarify urgency before interrupting focus.
- We will discuss capacity before overload becomes crisis.
- We will model recovery as part of professional performance.

## Workplace application challenge

Trial one meeting or messaging boundary for two weeks and review the impact.

# Module 8: Creating a team resilience charter

## Module purpose

To consolidate program learning into a practical charter the team can use.

## Learning outcomes

Participants will create a charter covering values, communication norms, support behaviours, meeting commitments, conflict repair, boundaries and accountability rhythms.

## Workplace scenario

A community organisation completes a team day and leaves motivated, but within three weeks old patterns return. The missing link is not inspiration; it is a visible charter, manager follow-up and review rhythm.

## Concept explanation

A team resilience charter is a working agreement. It should be short enough to use, specific enough to guide behaviour and flexible enough to update. The charter becomes valuable when it appears in meetings, onboarding, project kick-offs and review conversations.

## Discussion activity

What agreement would make the biggest difference to our team if we practised it consistently for 90 days?

## Team exercise: Charter build

Complete the charter templates in the asset pack. Select no more than ten commitments for the first version.

A charter should not replace employment contracts, policies or compliance obligations. It should make everyday team expectations clearer.

## Behaviour commitments

- We will choose fewer commitments and practise them well.
- We will review the charter monthly for the first quarter.
- We will use the charter to support new starters and project teams.

## Workplace application challenge

Book three monthly charter review conversations before leaving the workshop.

# Implementation roadmap for managers

## Week 1: Launch

Share the charter draft, confirm immediate behaviour commitments and add a five-minute resilience check to team meetings.

## Weeks 2-4: Practise

Use CLEAR updates for priorities, review one pressure pattern and test one boundary norm.

## Month 2: Embed

Discuss trust deposits and withdrawals, review support pathways and practise repair language after a low-risk tension.

## Month 3: Review

Repeat the team resilience diagnostic, update the charter and agree the next 90-day focus.

# Manager follow-up guide

Managers should model the charter, refer to it in meetings, invite feedback on what is and is not working, protect appropriate support pathways and avoid using the manual as a performance weapon. The most important follow-up is visible repetition: put commitments in agendas, one-on-ones, project launches and retrospectives.
